# Research Report

## Executive Summary

The pathophysiology of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is intrinsically linked to aberrant functional architecture within the Default Mode Network (DMN), manifesting primarily as pathological hyperconnectivity among key nodes such as the medial prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate cortex, which subserves maladaptive self-referential processing and rumination ([Zhou et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-025-03758-w)). This dysconnectivity is frequently associated with systemic low-grade inflammation, suggesting a neuroimmunological component to network dysfunction ([Aruldass et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2021.08.226)). Therapeutic neuromodulation strategies target these topological anomalies through distinct mechanisms; Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT) induces significant modulation of global brain network topology, characterized by the normalization of hyperconnectivity, although specific enhancements in DMN functional connectivity have also been correlated with positive clinical response ([Pang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2022.03.035)). Conversely, serotonergic psychedelics precipitate an acute disintegration of the DMN consistent with the "entropic brain" hypothesis, a phenomenon quantified by increased neural signal diversity and fractal dimension of cortical activity during the psychedelic state ([Varley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117049)). This acute network desegregation is thought to facilitate cognitive flexibility and has been associated with antidepressant efficacy in treatment-resistant populations ([Carhart-Harris et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-13282-7)).

The therapeutic mechanisms of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) involve more gradual or circuit-specific reconfigurations of large-scale brain networks. rTMS exerts its effects by normalizing aberrant interactions between the frontoparietal network (FPN) and the DMN, primarily through modulation of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex; however, treatment outcomes are significantly influenced by inter-individual variability in the functional connectivity profiles of stimulation targets (DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2014.01.023; DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2022.902089). Pharmacologically, SSRIs exhibit a complex temporal trajectory in DMN modulation, where acute administration typically reduces cross-regional DMN connectivity in a manner predicted by serotonin transporter occupancy (DOI: 10.1016/j.euroneuro.2018.07.099; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0068355), while longitudinal treatment results in a sustained reduction of connectivity in drug-naïve patients ([Li et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25391)). Despite these advancements, the development of robust clinical biomarkers for treatment prediction remains hindered by substantial methodological heterogeneity across neuroimaging studies ([Mao et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106120)). Although baseline intrinsic connectomes show promise in predicting remission ([Korgaonkar et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-019-0574-2)), the field requires standardized analytical pipelines to translate these network-based findings into reliable clinical tools for personalized psychiatry.

## Neurobiology of Default Mode Network Dysconnectivity in Major Depressive Disorder

The pathophysiology of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is intrinsically linked to the aberrant functional architecture of the Default Mode Network (DMN), a large-scale brain system subserving self-referential processing, autobiographical memory, and internal mentation. In clinical cohorts, baseline assessments have revealed significant DMN dysconnectivity, which is frequently characterized by pathological hyperconnectivity among key nodes such as the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), and precuneus ([Bai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2025.120491)). This hyperconnectivity is hypothesized to underlie the cognitive perseveration and maladaptive rumination observed in MDD, as patients exhibit an inability to disengage the DMN during tasks requiring externally directed attention. The specificity of these alterations is further underscored by subregional analyses of the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), which demonstrate symptom-specific intrinsic connectivity changes, suggesting that the pathophysiology is not uniform across the DMN but is topologically organized according to clinical phenomenology ([Zhou et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-025-03758-w)).

Contemporary models of depression emphasize the interaction between the DMN and the brain's reward circuitry. Research indicates that the dysconnectivity observed in MDD is not isolated to the default mode but involves a decoupling or aberrant interaction with reward networks, potentially contributing to the anhedonia and diminished positive affect characteristic of the disorder ([Bai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2025.120491)). This network-level dysfunction appears to be modulated by systemic biological factors, particularly inflammation. Studies have established a significant association between blood inflammatory markers and dysconnectivity in brain functional networks, positing that neuroimmune activation may drive the functional isolation or hyper-synchronization of DMN components ([Aruldass et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2021.08.226)). Specifically, the presence of systemic low-grade inflammation has been shown to exacerbate DMN and reward network dysconnectivity in young individuals with MDD, suggesting a distinct neurobiological subtype characterized by an inflammatory etiology ([Bai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2025.120491)).

Furthermore, the topological properties of these networks offer predictive insights into the longitudinal trajectory of the illness. Graph-theoretical analyses have demonstrated that individual variation in brain network topology is significantly linked to the course of illness in MDD, implying that specific patterns of DMN connectivity may serve as trait markers for disease chronicity or recovery potential rather than transient state markers ([Sheng et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhac015)). Collectively, these findings delineate a complex baseline pathophysiology in MDD where inflammatory processes interact with intrinsic network topology to disrupt the balance between self-referential and reward processing.

## Modulation of Global Network Topology and Metabolism following Electroconvulsive Therapy

Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) induces significant modulation of global brain network topology, primarily characterized by the normalization of aberrant connectivity patterns within the default mode network (DMN). In major depressive disorder, the DMN typically exhibits a pathological state of hyperconnectivity, which is associated with maladaptive self-referential processing and rumination. Comprehensive meta-analyses of neuromodulation studies demonstrate that successful ECT treatment is consistently associated with a reduction in this DMN functional connectivity ([Mao et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106120)). These alterations in functional architecture are accompanied by changes in global network efficiency, suggesting that ECT facilitates a topological reorganization away from the constrained, highly interconnected states observed in depression ([Brakowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2017.04.007)).

Critically, the therapeutic efficacy of ECT is closely linked to corresponding decreases in cerebral metabolism within the DMN. Neuroimaging evidence indicates that the reduction in functional connectivity parallels a downregulation of regional glucose metabolism and blood flow in key DMN nodes. The magnitude of this metabolic reduction has been shown to correlate significantly with symptom remission, suggesting that the clinical benefits of ECT are mediated by the attenuation of hypermetabolic activity in these networks ([Mao et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106120)). This relationship underscores the utility of functional connectivity metrics not only as markers of disease state but as predictors of treatment response. Research utilizing multivariate pattern recognition has confirmed that ECT-induced changes in brain functional connectivity can robustly predict therapeutic outcomes, reinforcing the value of network topology parameters in forecasting neuromodulation efficacy ([Li et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41537-017-0023-7)). While such predictive connectivity patterns have been documented across various neuropsychiatric conditions, the convergence of reduced DMN connectivity, lowered cerebral metabolism, and improved global efficiency highlights a specific mechanism of action for ECT in the remediation of depressive pathophysiology ([Brakowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2017.04.007)).

## Psychedelic-Induced Network Entropy and Acute Disintegration of the Default Mode Network

The acute administration of serotonergic psychedelics precipitates a profound reorganization of intrinsic brain networks, characterized most notably by the functional disintegration of the Default Mode Network (DMN). This phenomenon is central to the "entropic brain" hypothesis, which posits that the psychedelic state corresponds to a critical phase transition marked by increased neural signal diversity and a reduction in the system's functional segregation. Neuroimaging data confirm that agents such as LSD and psilocybin significantly increase the fractal dimension of cortical activity, reflecting a heightened complexity of neural signaling in both spatial and temporal domains ([Varley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117049)). Recent scoping reviews of the fMRI literature further substantiate that the suppression of DMN integrity is a reproducible hallmark of the psychedelic state, facilitating a breakdown of functional boundaries between typically segregated large-scale networks ([Beneš et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1606798)).

In the context of psilocybin administration, the magnitude of network desegregation is not uniform but demonstrates a specific pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic relationship. Research indicates that the degree of reduced network integrity and segregation within the DMN correlates directly with plasma psilocin levels, establishing a dose-dependent link between drug concentration and neurophysiological effect ([Madsen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2021.06.001)). Crucially, the extent of this acute network disintegration is predictive of the subjective psychedelic experience, where the loss of DMN segregation parallels the intensity of phenomenological effects reported by subjects ([Madsen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2021.06.001)). While these findings suggest a robust mechanism for therapeutic action via network resetting, the responsiveness of the DMN to psychedelic perturbation may be contingent upon baseline pathology. Preclinical translational research demonstrates that in alcohol-dependent populations, the characteristic psilocybin-induced DMN hypoconnectivity is significantly blunted compared to healthy controls, implying that chronic substance use alters the network's sensitivity to serotonergic modulation ([Reinwald et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-023-02690-1)). Consequently, while increased cortical entropy and DMN disintegration are generally associated with therapeutic outcomes, the capacity for such acute neuroplastic changes may be impaired in specific neuropsychiatric conditions.

## rTMS-Mediated Normalization of Frontoparietal and Default Mode Network Interactions

The therapeutic efficacy of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) in psychiatric and neurological disorders is increasingly understood through the lens of large-scale network modulation, specifically the normalization of aberrant interactions between the frontoparietal network (FPN) and the default mode network (DMN). Pathophysiological models of depression and cognitive impairment consistently implicate a failure of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC)—a core node of the FPN—to exert inhibitory control over the DMN, resulting in the DMN hyperconnectivity and reduced anti-correlation associated with maladaptive rumination and cognitive deficits ([Dhami et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaa004)). In this context, high-frequency rTMS applied to the DLPFC is posited to restore top-down executive control by suppressing DMN activity and re-establishing functional segregation between task-positive and task-negative networks. Seminal work utilizing functional connectivity analyses has demonstrated that successful clinical response to rTMS correlates with a decrease in subgenual cortex connectivity and the normalization of DMN connectivity patterns, effectively "resetting" the network dynamics that characterize the depressed brain ([Liston et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2014.01.023)). These findings are supported by sham-controlled trials which indicate that the modulation of connectivity between the FPN and DMN is a specific neurophysiological effect of active stimulation, distinguishing it from placebo responses and correlating with symptom reduction ([Taylor et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2018.02.019)).

The mechanism by which rTMS facilitates this network normalization involves complex interactions with the salience network (SN), which functions as a switch between the DMN and the central executive network. Research indicates that the baseline segregation of the SN is a robust predictor of treatment response, suggesting that the capacity of rTMS to modulate FPN-DMN coupling may be contingent upon the integrity of this switching mechanism ([Fan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2019.101719)). Furthermore, the efficacy of DLPFC stimulation is not uniform; it relies heavily on precise targeting that accounts for inter-individual variability in functional connectivity. Mapping studies have revealed substantial variability in the functional connectivity profiles of TMS targets, implying that standardized anatomical targeting may frequently miss the specific functional nodes necessary for optimal FPN-DMN modulation ([Harita et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.902089)). Consequently, personalized targeting strategies, such as those employing functional connectivity-guided neuronavigation, have shown enhanced efficacy. For instance, personalized theta burst stimulation protocols have been found to significantly improve clinical outcomes in conditions such as autism spectrum disorder, likely by refining the modulation of specific frontoparietal circuits involved in social cognition ([Xiao et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2025.01.002)).

This network normalization hypothesis extends beyond mood disorders to cognitive impairment and other neuropsychiatric conditions. In amnestic mild cognitive impairment (MCI), accelerated intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) has been shown to induce significant changes in resting-state functional connectivity that correspond temporally with cognitive improvement, suggesting that the restoration of network integrity is a transdiagnostic mechanism of cognitive recovery ([Aghamoosa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2025.04.012)). Systematic reviews of rTMS in MCI further support the notion that enhancement of cognitive functions is mediated by the modulation of these large-scale networks, particularly the strengthening of frontoparietal connectivity ([Sharbafshaaer et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm12196190)). Conversely, when network propagation is compromised, as seen in the breakdown of TMS-evoked EEG signal propagation within the DMN in Alzheimer's disease, the therapeutic potential of neuromodulation may be limited, highlighting the necessity of intact structural pathways for effective network modulation ([Maiella et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2024.09.007)).

The physiological underpinnings of rTMS-mediated network changes are multifaceted, involving both hemodynamic and electrophysiological components. Systematic reviews evidence that prefrontal TMS exerts instantaneous effects on brain oxygenation, implying that the normalization of FPN-DMN coupling is driven by immediate metabolic and vascular changes in the stimulated cortex and its connected regions ([Xia et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120618)). Moreover, rTMS appears to influence the dynamic reconfiguration of brain networks. In individuals with tobacco use disorder, stimulation has been shown to reshape the dynamic functional interactions between the executive and reward networks, demonstrating that rTMS can alter the temporal flexibility of network states ([Li et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.08.120)). This dynamic modulation is also relevant to behavioral regulation; low-frequency rTMS over the right DLPFC has been observed to modulate approach-avoidance behavior by altering the neural circuits underlying decision-making, further confirming the sensitivity of frontoparietal circuitry to electromagnetic stimulation ([Sütçübaşı et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2024.112392)).

The scope of rTMS network modulation is not limited to cortical function but extends to global physiological coupling. In chronic insomnia, where there is reduced coupling between global brain activity and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) flow, low-frequency rTMS has been demonstrated to enhance this coupling, suggesting that the therapeutic effects of stimulation may also involve the normalization of glymphatic clearance and global oscillatory rhythms ([Lu et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag016)). Similarly, in disorders of consciousness, high-frequency rTMS induces changes in resting-state functional connectivity that correlate with behavioral improvements, indicating that the stimulation of prefrontal nodes can facilitate the reintegration of large-scale networks necessary for consciousness ([Liu et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2018.00982)). Collectively, these findings underscore that the therapeutic utility of rTMS targeting the DLPFC lies in its ability to functionally reconnect the central executive network with the DMN, a mechanism that is observable across a diverse spectrum of neuropsychiatric pathologies, including depressive symptoms in schizophrenia ([Gallucci et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.20230655)).

## Pharmacodynamics of SSRIs: Longitudinal Alterations in DMN Functional Connectivity

The pharmacodynamic profile of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) on the Default Mode Network (DMN) is characterized by a complex temporal evolution, where immediate neurochemical occupancy precipitates acute functional alterations that differ significantly from the gradual restorative normalization observed over longitudinal treatment courses. Unlike rapid-acting neuromodulatory interventions which often induce immediate network reorganization, the therapeutic mechanism of SSRIs appears to involve a slower, cumulative modulation of synaptic connectivity. Acute administration of serotonergic agents elicits immediate, widespread alterations in functional brain architecture; for instance, single-dose fluoxetine in adolescents has been shown to induce significant changes in resting-state connectivity ([Capitão et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881120959608)), while acute intravenous citalopram rapidly modulates regional DMN connectivity ([Dutta et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-019-0447-0)). These acute effects are not confined to depressed cohorts, as single-dose serotonergic stimulation demonstrates widespread effects on functional connectivity in general populations ([Klaassens et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.08.012)). Mechanistically, these early shifts are contingent upon serotonin transporter (SERT) occupancy, wherein the degree of occupancy by citalopram directly predicts subsequent DMN connectivity patterns ([Schrantee et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2018.07.099)). Specifically, the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), a critical hub of the DMN, exhibits altered connectivity strength and stability following citalopram administration ([Arnone et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2018.01.021)), and escitalopram has been observed to decrease cross-regional functional connectivity within the DMN ([van de Ven et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068355)).

As the treatment course extends from the acute phase to longitudinal administration, the pharmacodynamic impact transitions toward the normalization of pathological hyperconnectivity, a process that aligns with the delayed onset of clinical therapeutic effects. In drug-naïve patients with major depressive disorder, an eight-week course of antidepressant treatment results in a significant reduction of functional connectivity, suggesting a downregulation of the excessive network synchrony often associated with the disorder ([Li et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25391)). This trajectory of decreasing connectivity over time is supported by systematic reviews of escitalopram, which document consistent alterations in functional connectivity throughout the treatment duration ([Turiaco et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/02698811251370998)). Extended treatment periods of twelve weeks reveal further modifications in the resting-state functional connectivity of large-scale brain networks ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.26231)), indicating that the restorative normalization of the DMN is a protracted process requiring sustained pharmacological exposure to facilitate treatment-induced network reorganization ([Han et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11682-026-01076-3)).

This gradual time-course of SSRI-induced connectivity normalization stands in stark contrast to the rapid neuroplastic reorganization associated with novel neuromodulatory therapies. While SSRIs operate through a slow, incremental modulation of serotonergic tone to eventually reduce DMN hyperconnectivity, rapid-acting agents such as psilocybin can induce immediate and predictive shifts in brain network dynamics ([Copa et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.02.089)). The distinction highlights that the "slower" pharmacodynamics of SSRIs may be necessary for the gradual consolidation of adaptive network changes, rather than the acute network dismantling seen with psychedelics. Despite the slow progression, baseline intrinsic connectome profiles remain predictive of remission ([Korgaonkar et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-019-0574-2)), even in late-life depression populations ([Ahmed et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291722003579)), suggesting that while the longitudinal trajectory is gradual, the potential for restorative DMN normalization is fundamentally anchored in pre-existing neural architecture.

## Methodological Heterogeneity and Clinical Biomarkers in Network-Based Treatment Prediction

The endeavor to establish robust clinical biomarkers for treatment prediction is significantly impeded by substantial methodological heterogeneity across neuroimaging studies, ranging from the selection of imaging modalities to the specific analytical pipelines employed. While the field theoretically encompasses a spectrum of modalities including PET and EEG, the current literature relies predominantly on resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI), creating a potential bias toward hemodynamic metrics over electrophysiological or receptor-level data. A recent coordinate-based meta-analysis of neuromodulation and psychotherapies for major depressive disorder (MDD) highlights that while functional connectivity patterns hold promise, the variance in study protocols complicates the identification of universal predictors ([Mao et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106120)). This variance is further compounded by the inconsistent application of structural versus functional metrics; for instance, distinct predictive signatures for antidepressant response have been identified when comparing structural connectivity against rs-fMRI, suggesting that a single-modality approach may be insufficient ([Wang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2024.02.004)).

Regarding analytical approaches, the field remains divided between seed-based correlation analysis and independent component analysis (ICA), with a growing trend toward multivariate pattern analysis. Early systematic reviews noted a lack of consensus in MDD findings, largely attributed to these varying methodological protocols which affect the reproducibility of connectivity metrics ([Dichter et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2014.09.028)). Subsequent efforts to reconcile these discrepancies using coordinate-based meta-analyses have sought to isolate consistent neural predictors of treatment improvement ([Long et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2020.06.072)). The complexity of analysis is further evidenced by advanced modeling techniques, such as structural equation modeling, which reveals that personality traits like neuroticism and openness exhibit anti-correlation patterns with dissociable default mode network (DMN) subsystems, implying that predictive models must account for trait-level neural architecture ([Zhi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11682-024-00869-8)). Furthermore, the shift toward multivariate approaches has demonstrated utility in predicting response to cognitive behavioral therapy in obsessive-compulsive disorder, offering superior predictive power over traditional univariate methods ([Reggente et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1716686115)).

Within this heterogeneous landscape, baseline connectivity of the Default Mode Network (DMN) has emerged as a recurring, albeit complex, predictive biomarker for differential treatment response. In the context of rapid-acting interventions, anterior DMN connectivity, specifically its relationship with the posterior insula, has been shown to predict depressive symptom reduction following serial ketamine infusion ([Wade et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291722001313)). Similarly, in the domain of psychedelic therapy, baseline functional connectivity has been successfully utilized to predict outcomes following psilocybin treatment for depression, corroborating earlier findings regarding the drug's mechanistic action on brain networks (DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2024.02.089; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-13282-7). For conventional neuromodulation, enhanced DMN functional connectivity has been robustly linked to positive responses to electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) ([Pang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2022.03.035)).

However, the specificity of DMN metrics as a biomarker is complicated by its involvement in diverse pathologies and its interaction with other large-scale networks. In late-life depression, for example, the interplay between the DMN and the cognitive control network appears critical, rather than DMN activity in isolation ([Alexopoulos et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2011.12.002)). Moreover, DMN dysregulation is not exclusive to mood disorders; it is also observed in conditions such as insomnia, where alterations in DMN and frontal control network connectivity are prevalent ([Zheng et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/cns.14183)). This suggests that baseline DMN connectivity may reflect a general susceptibility to neuropsychiatric dysfunction or a transdiagnostic symptom profile rather than a specific predictor of treatment response for a single condition. The predictive scope of network connectivity extends beyond the DMN to other functional axes; for instance, cerebellar-cortical connectivity has been identified as a predictor of antipsychotic treatment response in first-episode psychosis ([Velioglu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sbaf021)), and resting-state connectivity predicts outcomes in neurological rehabilitation, such as language therapy in post-stroke aphasia ([Falconer et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2023.11.022)) and recovery after cardiac arrest ([Wagner et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-68683-y)).

The issue of heterogeneity also pervades non-psychiatric applications, which often inform psychiatric biomarker discovery. In pain management, resting-state connectivity has been shown to predict motor cortex stimulation-dependent pain relief in fibromyalgia ([Argaman et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-21557-x)), while other studies have demonstrated that connectivity profiles can differentiate between drug and placebo responders ([Schmidt-Wilcke et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2014.09.007)). Even novel interventions like mechanical affective touch therapy for anxiety induce measurable changes in resting-state functional connectivity ([Gonsalves et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurom.2021.10.007)). Ultimately, while the integration of network-based biomarkers offers a promising avenue for personalized medicine, the field must address the significant methodological discordance between analytical techniques and modality selection to translate these findings into clinically reliable tools (DOI: 10.1016/j.schres.2021.09.004; DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.18101160).
